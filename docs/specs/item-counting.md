## Problem

Owners, managers and workers see inconsistent production totals. Unframed prints are excluded from Items, even though they are physical output. Sets already have a multiplier, but the browser and digest have different SKU parsers and counting loops. Reports counts orders from verified lines, whereas other views use completion events. Production reload counts repeated completion rows and can include excluded items; Welcome misses verification cancellations. A change to the eligibility predicate alone would preserve these disagreements and could incorrectly include unframed work in frame-time averages.

F1 must give Reports, Welcome, the Production live counter and the authenticated daily digest the same results for the same saved evidence, location, person/work-type scope and reporting window. A count is production work credit, not an inventory movement or a shipment count.

The reporting-scale amendment addresses repeated calculation and transfer as history grows. CJ expects 200 orders per site per day with three physical items per order: 1,800 items/day across three sites, 12,600/week and 657,000/year if operating every day. Items, order lines and event rows are different units: quantities can cover multiple pieces, and work stages, completions, corrections and clocks add events. Capacity evidence must record all of these separately. A slow historical read is not evidence that database storage is full.

## Goals

- Count eligible physical pieces separately from distinct completed orders, including unframed prints and set quantities.
- Use one browser/server-compatible pure item-counting function and one shared effective-event counting contract across every affected summary and drill-down.
- Handle cancelled verifications, repeated completion events, rework and incomplete history explicitly.
- Use the approved site-local production day consistently, and make partial-day/different-scope comparisons understandable.
- Recompute historical views from saved events without rewriting history; explain the changed totals in release notes and report labels.
- Retain the digest-auth protection merged in PR #6 and existing inventory, routing and production capture behavior.
- Serve management counts from stored, rebuildable summaries calculated on the server with the shared counting rules. Report opening must not download months of raw events or start a rebuild.
- Keep successful worker actions immediately visible while management views update periodically, with an explicit data cutoff and freshness status.
- Recalculate affected history after new events or corrections, preserve exact distinct Orders, and prove recovery and performance at the growth workload in section E.

## Non-goals

- No scanner hardware/session/offline outbox work, department administration, new reporting permissions, timing-method redesign, Shopify timeline or Make/Slack integration. Those remain F5–F12 or shared prerequisites identified in the review. Durable server-side reporting work and summary storage are now in scope; they do not implement F6 offline capture.
- No new canvas credit, fuzzy SKU recognition, price-based classification, catalogue cleanup, guessed quantities or retroactive worker/team reassignment.
- No stock or packaging deduction changes, automatic data repair, event deletion or new production write/idempotency system.
- No changes to existing digest schedules, recipients or S1 authentication. A protected summary refresh schedule and authenticated summary/detail reads are in scope. No anonymous report or rebuild endpoint, production access during development/testing, or additional database/analytics vendor is authorized by this spec.
- No full F8 trends/summary-export redesign. Existing KPI displays and calculated export values must be consistent; existing raw event exports retain their raw quantities and timestamps.

## User stories / flows

- As a worker, I verify two units of an unframed two-piece set and see four Items. After saving a qualifying order completion, Orders increases by one. Refreshing or changing away and back does not change either result.
- As an owner or manager, I select a site, person/work type and period in Reports and reconcile its totals with the equivalent Welcome/live/digest scope. Department/work-type order subtotals can overlap; the overall distinct order total is not their sum.
- As a worker, I undo a verification and see only that verification's item credit removed. A later reload or digest applies the same correction.
- As CJ, I can distinguish normal output, identifiable CS/rework output and incomplete historical coverage without guessing missing facts.
- As CJ, I can download a staging digest with the staging secret and verify counts without sending email or weakening authentication.
- As a manager, I can open a historical report without loading its entire event history. I see when its data was last verified and whether an update is delayed.
- As CJ, I can rebuild stored summaries from original records, compare results, and restore reporting after a failed job without changing stock or replaying production actions.

## Acceptance criteria

### A. What counts as an item

- [ ] A normalized, recognized single-print SKU with variant `FB`, `FW`, `FN` or `UN` contributes its saved product quantity. A framed print is one item, not a print plus a frame.
- [ ] A recognized set-print SKU with one of those variants contributes `product quantity × set piece count`. Supported set counts are the existing single-digit positive counts 2–9, including `GW` size composites. Reject invalid counts such as S0/S1 for this contract; do not silently turn malformed sets into singles. General routing/parser expansion is outside scope.
- [ ] Recognized frame-only `PF` products with variants `PB`, `PW` or `PN` continue to count as one item per unit. They are not the excluded accessories.
- [ ] Gift cards, free-print SKUs (including recognized current `INKA-AUX…` and legacy `INKA-AUX-FP-…` forms), hooks/other accessories and packaging contribute zero items. The same exclusion applies when such a line appears in a mixed order.
- [ ] Canvas (`SC`) remains excluded in F1; it needs separate explicit approval before becoming eligible. Unknown/missing SKUs, unsupported formats and malformed set codes contribute no guessed pieces and are identified as unclassified coverage.
- [ ] Quantities must be finite nonnegative safe integers with a safe-integer multiplication result. Zero contributes zero; missing/null, negative, fractional, nonfinite or overflowing values are uncountable and flagged. Do not default missing historical quantities to one or silently clamp negative totals.
- [ ] Normalize case and surrounding whitespace consistently. Browser and server use the same classifier/counting function, including explicit `counted`, `excluded` or `unclassified` outcomes and a reason where useful. There must not be a second digest copy of the business rules.

| Saved verified line | Product quantity | Items |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| `INKA-S2BON0064-A1-UN` | 2 | 4 |
| `INKA-S3WIL0049-A2-FN` | 2 | 6 |
| `INKA-KAN0566-A4-FB` | 3 | 3 |
| `INKA-KAN0566-A4-UN` | 3 | 3 |
| `INKA-PF0004-A0-PW` | 2 | 2 |
| Recognized six-piece GW set, framed or UN | 1 | 6 |
| Gift card, free print, accessory or box | any valid quantity | 0, excluded |
| Canvas | any valid quantity | 0, excluded in F1 |
| Missing/unknown SKU or eligible SKU with null quantity | unknown | No guessed count; coverage warning |

### B. Saved evidence, orders and rework

- [ ] Items come from effective `line_verified` events, at the verification time and with the recorded person, site, work type, SKU and quantity. A `started`, `completed` or `dispatched` event does not add its order-level `line_quantity` to Items. Items may therefore appear before order completion.
- [ ] A `cancelled` event with a valid `parent_event_id` removes that parent's verification contribution once, using the parent's saved quantity. Apply cancellation resolution before person/work-type/display-period filtering. A cancellation saved later may change an earlier period; it does not create negative production in the cancellation day. An orphan/mismatched parent remains a coverage issue, not an arbitrary subtraction. A line cancellation does not automatically erase a separate order completion.
- [ ] Repeated retrieval of the same `(location, event id)` is counted once. Do not collapse distinct verification events just because order/SKU/person match: the current data lacks a stable line/attempt ID and may represent two lines or real repeat work. If apparently duplicated legacy rows cannot be resolved from saved evidence, flag ambiguity rather than claim retry-safe or uniquely reconstructed history. F1 adds no writes that create further event duplicates.
- [ ] **Completion definition:** Orders uses `completed` for the three production work types and `dispatched` for dispatch, with a nonblank saved order identity and valid saved site/work type. A verification by itself is not a completed order. Resolve any explicit terminal-event reversal by its saved parent reference; never infer an order cancellation from a line undo alone.
- [ ] Within normal output, count an order once per site/work type/selected report period. Repeated completion rows do not increase it. The overall count is a distinct union of `(site, order number)` across included work types, never a sum of work-type or daily distinct counts. A site boundary prevents the same displayed number at another site from colliding.
- [ ] Person Orders counts that person's qualifying saved terminal events; person Items uses that person's effective verifications. Do not transfer credit to today's owner, most recent verifier or current team. Personal/work-type totals can overlap and must not be presented as additive distinct-order totals.
- [ ] **Qualification context:** for each terminal event, inspect original saved `line_verified` rows with the same site, order number, work type and CS case (including null for normal work), whose event time is at or before that terminal's time and which exist in the report's data snapshot. Include all recorded workers in that context before applying the display's person filter; never join another work type/site/case or later verification. Terminal-row quantities/SKUs are not substitute line evidence. At least one recognized eligible product in this context establishes eligible completion; missing/bad quantities still require an item-coverage warning.
- [ ] An order completion with absent or unclassified line context counts as a distinct completed order but carries incomplete/uncertain item coverage. Only a nonempty context made exclusively of positively classified excluded products disqualifies it. Do not treat failed/unauthorized context retrieval as an empty context: show unavailable/limited coverage instead. This conservative historical rule does not infer individual completion attempts from order/SKU matches; retain that limitation when multiple attempts share the available identity.
- [ ] Qualification uses original line evidence, not the effective item sum after cancellation. Thus a mixed order remains one completed order when its eligible verification is later undone, even if only accessory verifications remain effective; its Items decrease and its correction is visible. Only an explicit reversal of the terminal removes that completion. A verification whose event time is after the terminal cannot qualify the earlier completion. This separates completed-order evidence from corrected piece credit without inventing an attempt linkage.
- [ ] Events with `cs_case_id` are a separate CS/rework group, with the same piece and distinct-order rules, not silently added to normal output. Its order key includes case ID so separate documented replacement cases can be distinguished. All four consumers expose normal and CS/rework totals, or an explicit separate labeled detail. Untagged repeated work cannot be reliably classified as rework; show that limitation instead of inventing a case or adding repeated Orders.
- [ ] An all-work-type Items total is the sum of credited pieces processed at the included work types; the same physical piece handled at two work types can contribute twice. Label the scope as work processed, not unique products manufactured or shipped. Unique physical-item tracking is outside the evidence available to F1. Keep per-work-type detail available.

### C. Reporting windows and consistency

- [ ] Store/interpret event times as UTC instants. Use the site's IANA timezone: AU `Australia/Sydney`, UK `Europe/London`, US `America/Chicago`. A production date D means `[04:00 on D, 04:00 on D+1)` in that zone. Convert each local boundary independently; do not add a fixed 24 UTC hours across DST transitions.
- [ ] Date-range filters include each selected production date; custom end dates convert to the next day's 04:00 exclusive boundary. Existing weekly comparisons use Monday-start local weeks. No new F8 calendar UI is required, but existing comparison values must use the same boundaries as their displayed totals.
- [ ] Live/current-day results are as of their data snapshot, with the production date and scope available to the reader. Digest `date=D` uses the same period; without a date, use the current production date at that site. Preserve the three existing digest cron expressions; the new summary refresh schedule is separate. A scheduled send before 04:00 closing time is an as-of report, not a claim that the full production day has ended. Historical re-downloads can differ after later activity/corrections; label the window, data cutoff and generation time.
- [ ] Reports, Welcome and digest management totals use a published summary version. Production action confirmation and the current worker counter can use newer committed evidence, clearly distinguished from the management data cutoff. All consumers share the same rules and agree exactly for equal evidence, visibility and scope; different cutoffs must not be presented as a discrepancy-free comparison. Distinct Orders over a longer range are computed from qualifying order identities in that range, never by adding daily order totals.
- [ ] Adding UN Items does not add UN observations or pieces to frame-time, per-frame cost/effort or framing benchmark denominators. Separate item eligibility from timing eligibility; retain current frame-only timing behavior until F9. Do not silently relabel a new mixed-output rate as a framing rate.
- [ ] Display unclassified/missing-detail counts and an incomplete-coverage indication where totals are shown, including the digest. Expected exclusions are distinguished from broken/missing data. If fetch/pagination fails, show an unavailable/incomplete state rather than a valid-looking zero or silently truncated total. Failed digest reads return an error before email generation/sending; bounded known data-quality gaps can produce a clearly labeled report.

### D. Regression and release gates

- [ ] Source interpretation remains read-only: summary generation writes only derived reporting state and its job/change-tracking state. Existing saved SKU/quantity/person/time values, stock/ledger rows, cancellation semantics, clocks, routing and package selection remain unchanged. A bounded durable change-capture mechanism may be added by the amended plan, but no synchronous summary calculation or external call may enter the production save path. Tests demonstrate the same inventory deduction for a set before/after F1, not an extra deduction for its KPI piece multiplier.
- [ ] Digest auth runs before database/report work. Preserve secret-only access, recipient allowlist validation, method/error precedence and no-store responses, including diagnostics and `dry=1`; all existing S1 tests continue to pass.
- [ ] Release notes state that UN output now counts, historical totals are rebuilt from saved facts, Orders uses completed/deduplicated evidence, CS/rework is separate, production-day boundaries are consistent, and management totals update periodically. Initial derived-summary backfill must not rewrite source history or manufacture missing facts.

### E. Stored summaries, freshness and scale

- [ ] **Refresh contract:** schedule summary refresh every 15 minutes. Under the accepted workload and healthy dependencies, committed changes must be reflected in published management reports within 30 minutes. These are separate from the existing report load-time targets. Show the last verified data cutoff, publication time and production period. At over 30 minutes of unprocessed change/freshness lag, show `Update delayed`; at two hours or more, show `Report out of date`. Failed jobs or missing summaries show an explicit error/unavailable state. Never mark old data current merely because the page reopened or a job started.
- [ ] **Worker feedback:** a saved verification, undo or completion receives immediate confirmation through the existing capture flow, without waiting for the reporting worker. A failed save never appears saved. The worker's current-action/current-day display uses bounded authorized evidence and the same rules; replay, reload, identity change and midnight/04:00 rollover must not double-count an optimistic update. Background report failure does not block a valid capture. This is not an offline capture guarantee.
- [ ] **Summary reads:** management Items, Orders, normal/rework breakdowns, coverage counts and their existing comparison values are returned as compact server results for the requested site/date/person/work-type scope. No raw-history payload or in-browser full-period reduction is needed to render them. Detail pages fetch only requested authorized records, with stable pagination and an explicit completion/error contract; a page limit must never limit a total silently. Frame-time, cost and attendance values retain their existing definitions and must not leave a hidden full-history download on the same report screen; the plan must account for each existing report metric's read path.
- [ ] **Exact identity:** retain enough derived contribution and qualifying-terminal identity to answer arbitrary existing date/person/work-type filters, exact distinct order unions and coverage deduplication. Daily item totals alone are insufficient. Two days or workers sharing an order still give one overall Order; separate sites and CS cases retain their existing keys. Coverage issues/notes, uncertain completions and timing inputs must preserve their scope, rather than being copied into every bucket.
- [ ] **One rule implementation:** background processing reuses the shared TypeScript classifier, effective-event preparation and counting semantics. SQL may select, persist and combine already-classified facts and exact identities, but must not become a second independent implementation of product eligibility, cancellation or completion qualification. Derived results must match the existing reducer over the same source snapshot and permission scope.
- [ ] **Complete change tracking:** new events, backdated inserts, valid late cancellations, existing authorized updates/deletes and changes to an event's old/new identity must invalidate every affected contribution, order, day and timing boundary. Include an orphan cancellation whose parent arrives later and future-dated evidence when it becomes eligible. Do not rely on `created_at > last_run`, a fixed recent-day lookback, or a maximum allocated sequence ID to infer committed completeness. The amended plan must prove commit-safe capture and identify affected old and new scopes without recurring whole-history scans.
- [ ] **Safe processing:** every reporting write has a deterministic identity/version and retry behavior. Duplicate scheduling, retries after an unknown commit result, concurrent workers, lease expiry and a crash between reading and publishing must not add credit twice, lose pending work or publish part of a change. Replace/rebuild affected contributions from saved facts rather than incrementing unverified counters. A failed job remains recoverable from durable state.
- [ ] **Consistent publication:** a report uses one coherent published generation with a verified source boundary and counting-rule version. Totals, comparisons and detail must either use that generation or explicitly require a refresh if it expired. New source changes during a build remain queued for the next generation. Publish replacements atomically only after required work and validation succeed; an incomplete backfill is never a complete site report. A generation identifier does not itself prove source completeness; document and test the boundary protocol.
- [ ] **Rebuild and retention:** keep original evidence; no source deletion or archival policy is introduced. Initial backfill and later rebuilds are resumable, run with bounded work per invocation, and write isolated derived generations. Reconcile Items, distinct Orders and coverage before promotion. Interrupted rebuilds leave the previous generation readable and labelled with its true cutoff. A rule change forces a versioned rebuild; do not mix old and new rule versions. The plan must specify derived-generation retention and expiry behavior separately from source retention.
- [ ] **Permission parity:** stored results are not a way around source permissions. A staff/self-limited result must not be produced by simply filtering a full-site aggregate that used hidden correction/qualification context. Use the caller's permitted evidence semantics and retain limited-coverage labels. Current role and site membership are checked on every summary and detail request; membership removal takes effect without waiting for a summary refresh. Job state, source snapshots and rebuild functions are private to the worker/operator.
- [ ] **Digest:** use a complete, compatible published generation for all count sheets and comparisons, with its cutoff printed in the workbook. With missing, failed or over-30-minute-stale publication coverage for a requested scope, return an error before generating/sending email; do not silently fall back to a large raw scan or an unlabelled old report. Preserve recipients, cron timing, `dry=1`, diagnostics and S1 auth/error precedence. Digest delivery retries remain governed by the existing delivery contract, not a new email guarantee.
- [ ] **Growth workload:** retain the existing two-year/three-site fixtures and add a five-year baseline at 200 orders/site/operating day, three pieces/order and 365 operating days/year (1,095,000 orders and 3,285,000 physical pieces across all sites). Generate realistic multiple work stages, quantities/sets, clocks, repeated completions, corrections, missing detail and rework; record resulting event/contribution counts, bytes and assumptions. Separately test a 10-times-volume busy day across all sites against that history and 20 concurrent authenticated report readers. The proposal does not claim that every physical piece requires its own event row.
- [ ] **Acceptance measurements:** retain one-day report load within 2 seconds and three-month summary load within 10 seconds after authentication, including server work, transfer and client rendering preparation. Measure three runs per role/site/scope and each reader's paired median under the 20-reader workload; all results must be complete. Record cold/first, median, slowest, payload, database work and errors. Also prove 30-minute publication latency at baseline and the 10-times busy-day load, interrupted-job recovery, resource/storage growth, and scan-save latency under background load. Reporting must not introduce capture failures; proposed capture p95 regression limit is 10% against the same workload with the worker disabled. Any failed limit blocks release rather than raising timeouts or shrinking fixtures.

### Approaches considered

Continue optimizing full-event reads: least change to the completed F1 work, but every long-range request still transfers and reduces detailed history. Suitable as an independent bounded correctness oracle, not the management read path selected here.

Calculate aggregates on demand in SQL: smaller responses, but repeated scans and a second implementation of correction/qualification rules risk cost and disagreement. Not selected as the primary calculation design.

**Selected direction, approved by CJ: durable background processing with stored reporting projections in the existing PostgreSQL/Supabase stack.** It reuses the shared rule implementation, rebuilds affected facts, and serves compact summaries plus on-demand details. It adds jobs, derived storage and operational checks, with eventual consistency that fits CJ's approximately two-hour management checks. A separately hosted warehouse, streaming platform or additional vendor is not needed by this spec; any demonstrated platform limit goes into the plan's Open questions.

## Hardening

### 1. Failure modes

Network/Supabase/job failures retain the last coherent publication with its true cutoff or show unavailable, never a successful zero. Background worker downtime must not prevent valid source saves; no remote calls or aggregation run inside capture transactions. If the plan uses a transactional change journal and its required local write fails, the source action must fail visibly and remain retryable rather than acknowledge an untracked save. The plan must measure that journal's overhead and provide a safe operational disable/recovery procedure. Duplicate requests, workers and unknown commit outcomes must be idempotent for derived state. Jobs have durable retry/lease/checkpoint state; failed publication is never partial success. Shopify/Dropbox/Make/Slack remain outside the counting dependency chain. Existing capture input validation and legacy duplicate limitations remain unchanged.

### 2. Data correctness

Use one piece-count/effective-event implementation for direct verification, background projection and parity tests. Preserve saved attribution, exact instants and site-local 04:00/DST boundaries. Keep source identities behind derived Items, exact distinct Orders and scoped coverage. Late changes replace affected historical contributions, including parent/terminal dependencies; they do not apply arbitrary negative credit to the arrival day. Version rules and publications, reconcile isolated rebuilds and never treat a job start time or largest event timestamp as a completeness proof. Do not invent missing quantities, collapse ambiguous saved events, change framing-time eligibility, or confuse work-stage output with unique physical stock.

### 3. Security

Authenticate every new read, schedule and operator entry point before work; validate site, dates, filters, generation and page tokens. ### 5. Performance

Management reads use indexed derived facts/summary aggregates; transfer is bounded by the requested result, not raw historical event volume. Existing distinct-order filters require identity-aware aggregation rather than additive daily counts. Background processing normally reads changed partitions and necessary dependencies; full scans belong to bounded initial rebuild/reconciliation jobs, not every 15-minute cycle.
